The 4 Laws of Behavior Change

Clear identifies four components of every habit: a cue, a craving, a response, and a reward. To build a good habit, make each law favorable. To break a bad one, invert each law.

1

Make it Obvious (Cue)

Design your environment so good behavior is the default choice. In Time Chart: Smart Notifications surface your daily tasks at the exact moment you're most likely to act.

2

Make it Attractive (Craving)

Bundle the habit with something enjoyable. In Time Chart: Battle Arena gamification and social leaderboards create natural positive anticipation for task completion.

3

Make it Easy (Response)

Reduce friction until the barrier to action is zero. In Time Chart: Micro-scheduling breaks tasks into sub-tasks so starting requires minimal cognitive energy.

4

Make it Satisfying (Reward)

Immediate reinforcement seals the habit loop. In Time Chart: Streak counters and instant audio completion feedback deliver immediate dopamine hits.

Identity-Based Habits: Who Do You Want to Be?

Most habit apps focus on outcomes (“I want to run a marathon”). Clear argues the deepest habits are built on identity (“I am a runner”). Every time you complete a task, you cast a vote for the person you want to become.
